\subsection{Support-vector machine (from scratch)}
\textit{Objective.} For Task~B we train a linear soft-margin SVM
(\texttt{PegasosSVM} in \texttt{src/svm.py}, NumPy only) on labels
$y_i\in\{-1,+1\}$ (any two class labels are mapped and mapped back). It
minimises the regularised hinge-loss primal
\begin{equation}
  J(\mathbf{w},b)=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\big(\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert^{2}+b^{2}\big)
  +\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max\!\big(0,\,1-y_i(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_i+b)\big).
  \label{eq:svm}
\end{equation}
Following the Pegasos box, the bias is the weight of a constant-1 feature,
so it is shrunk like $\mathbf{w}$; this adds the $b^{2}$ term, which is
negligible on standardized features and stops the large first steps
($\eta_1=1/\lambda$) from inflating $b$.

\textit{Solver.} Pegasos~\cite{pegasos}: each epoch visits a fresh random
permutation of the rows in mini-batches $A_t$ of size $k$ and applies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}\leftarrow(1-\eta_t\lambda)\,\mathbf{w}
  +\frac{\eta_t}{k}\sum_{i\in A_t^{+}} y_i\mathbf{x}_i,
  \quad A_t^{+}=\{i\in A_t: y_i(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_i+b)<1\},
\end{equation}
with the same rule for $b$, followed by an optional projection of
$(\mathbf{w},b)$ onto the ball of radius $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$. $k=1$ is the
classic per-example update (our default); $k=n$ is the full-batch
sub-gradient method. Mini-batches replace a Python step per row with one
vectorised step per batch; $k=1$ keeps a scalar path and gives
bit-identical results to the per-example solver. The objective
\eqref{eq:svm} is recorded after every epoch.

\textit{Hyperparameters.} We expose $\lambda$ (equivalently
$C=1/(\lambda n)$), the number of epochs, the batch size, and three
learning-rate schedules: Pegasos $\eta_t=1/(\lambda t)$, inverse square
root $\eta_t=\eta_0/\sqrt{t}$, and constant $\eta_t=\eta_0$ (we require
$\eta_0\lambda<1$ so the shrink factor stays positive). Features are
imputed and standardized with training statistics only, since the hinge
loss is scale-sensitive.

\textit{Margin.} After fitting we report $\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert$, the
margin width $2/\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert$, and the support vectors, i.e.\
the training points with $y_i(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_i+b)\le 1$.

\textit{Non-linearity: random Fourier features.} \texttt{RFFPegasosSVM}
approximates the RBF kernel $k(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')=
\exp(-\gamma\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'\rVert^{2})$ with
$\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})=\sqrt{2/D}\cos(W^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{c})$,
$W_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,2\gamma)$, $c_k\sim\mathcal{U}(0,2\pi)$~\cite{rff},
so $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x}')\approx
k(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')$, and trains the unchanged linear solver on
$\boldsymbol{\phi}(X)$ ($D=500$). We chose RFF over kernelised Pegasos
because the kernel version keeps one coefficient per training row and
needs kernel evaluations against all of them at every step: $O(n^{2})$
per epoch (about $2\times10^{9}$ for our $\approx$45k training rows) and
$O(n)$ per prediction. RFF costs $O(nD)$ per epoch, predicts in $O(D)$,
and reuses the tested linear code. One consequence: since
$\lVert\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\rVert^{2}\approx 1$ while a
standardized $\mathbf{x}$ has $\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^{2}\approx 71$, the
RFF model needs its own (smaller) $\lambda$.

\textit{Tests.} \texttt{tests/test\_svm.py} checks the label mapping,
determinism, a decreasing objective, the margin and support-vector
definitions, a closed-form full-batch first step, that RFF approximates
the RBF Gram matrix and separates concentric circles where the linear
model cannot, and agreement with scikit-learn's
\texttt{SGDClassifier(loss="hinge")} on a toy split.

\subsection{SVM: weights, schedule and gap to scikit-learn}
All numbers in this subsection are on the validation set
(\texttt{python -m src.experiments.svm\_analysis}, $\lambda=10^{-3}$, the
best linear $\lambda$ by validation F1).

\textit{Weights.} On standardized features the largest positive
(premium) weights are \texttt{rooms} (+1.65) and \texttt{area} (+1.33),
then central Baku locations (Sabail district +0.54, the Shah Ismail
Khatai and Ag Sheher areas) and the new-build category (+0.44).
The most negative are Khachmaz district ($-0.64$), the land category
($-0.44$), Absheron and Surakhani districts, and the old-build
category. Because \texttt{rooms} and \texttt{area}
are correlated, and the one-hot blocks are collinear with the bias,
these are conditional effects that split credit between related
features, not independent importances.

\textit{Epochs and schedule.} With the Pegasos schedule the objective
falls from 0.556 after one epoch to 0.389 after 20, and the margin
$2/\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert$ widens from 0.41 to 0.74 while validation F1
rises from 0.811 to 0.841. $\eta_t=\eta_0/\sqrt{t}$ ($\eta_0=0.5$)
converges fastest (0.399 after one epoch). A constant step
($\eta_0=0.01$) stalls at $J\approx0.45$ with a narrower margin
($\approx0.56$) and F1 0.82--0.83: without a decaying step, SGD keeps
bouncing at its noise level.

\textit{Gap to scikit-learn.} Table~\ref{tab:svm_sklearn}: LinearSVC
solves the same problem to (near) optimality, and our objective is about
3\% above it after 10 epochs; the weight vectors point the same way
(cosine similarity 0.994) and 95.9\% of validation predictions agree.
F1 is the same to within 0.002; AUC is about 0.005 lower. The gap comes from
(i) a stochastic solver stopped after 10 epochs, whose $1/(\lambda t)$
step is still noisy, (ii) regularising the bias, which LinearSVC also
does but SGDClassifier does not, and (iii) the projection step. Ours is
the slowest because the per-example update is a Python loop while
scikit-learn's solvers are compiled; mini-batches of 256 cut our fit to
about 0.3\,s (10 epochs) and, with 50 epochs, match the per-example F1
with AUC 0.921.
